## Protocol

- One node process per candidate per round (no JIT warmth shared between candidates). Three rounds, candidates interleaved 0,1,2,3,4,5 per round.
- Per valid (N, L): one untimed warm-up call (seed 1), then seeds 1..20 each timed with performance.now() around buildPuzzle(N, seed, L, 30). The generator path itself has no clock, no Math.random, no node budget in the level logic.
- Bounds: NFR-1 200 ms (4,1); NFR-2 500 ms (6,L); NFR-3 3000 ms (8,L). Target (A-36 tripwire) = 50% of the bound: 100 / 250 / 1500 ms. "share of target" = worst / target; at most 100% meets the tripwire.
- MACHINE LOAD WARNING: the dev machine was heavily loaded by other processes during the runs (IDE, browser, other sessions): 1-minute load average 125 down to 10 on 10 cores, printed per round below. Absolute numbers are inflated and noisy, most in round 1; the per-candidate maximum over the three rounds is reported as the conservative figure. Round 3 of candidate 0 (load 31: 6x6 L4 345 ms) reproduces the earlier prototype read (359 ms). Task 2.9 must re-measure on the real code.
- Attempts, run-outs and givens are deterministic and identical in all rounds.

## Candidates as implemented (cumulative)

- 0: the prototype (docs/qa/difficulty-measurement/proto.ts.txt) with cap 4 and the pinned tie-breaks of decision 4 (t2 changed to cell-outer, digit-inner; t3 lowest agreeing complete line, first empty cell; t4 fewest steps, then row, column, v = 0 first). Technique 1 through today's hint() (sentences built), findViolations after the placement and after each step, two board copies per look-ahead candidate.
- (a): sentence-free shared nextFill(board, ceiling) on the Grid (technique 1 re-implemented: rule-outer pair, sandwich, count; then axis, line; pair target i-1 before i+2; count target the first empty), one copy per look-ahead candidate, no wasted 5th step. The walk (solves) still runs findViolations before every step, like today's solveByRules via hint.
- (b): stop the look-ahead scan at the first 0-step candidate.
- (c): local violation check (the row and the column through the cell just set: triple, count, duplicate against every other complete line of that axis) instead of findViolations inside the look-ahead; in the walk, one full findViolations at the start, then a local check after each fill. Exact because the board before each single change is violation-free and every check follows exactly one change.
- (d): flat Int8Array board (-1 empty) with an empties counter; look-ahead places the value and the propagated fills on the board and undoes them from a trail (no copy per candidate). Note: (d) also drops the per-line closures that the Grid versions (a)-(c) allocate, so its gain is not "undo" alone; the two are not separated here.
- (e): for L = 2..4 only, per attempt one shuffle (no extra draw), pass 1 carves over all positions with the predicate "walk at L-1 solves", pass 2 goes over the remaining givens in the same shuffled order with "walk at L solves"; accept iff pass 2 removed at least one cell AND the walk at L-1 does not solve (the L-1 rejection test is kept). Level 1 is unchanged.

Why it regresses: after pass 1 the board is already maximal for ceiling L-1, so pass 2 succeeds only if some remaining given is removable at L; at (6,2) and (6,4) that set is often empty, so attempts and run-outs go up. No change to FR-83/FR-84 wording is needed, because (e) is not chosen.

## Follow-up (orchestrator request, same day): attempt bound over seeds 1..1000, construction (a)+(b)+(c)+(d)

Run: `CAND=4 MODE=stuck npx tsx spike.ts` and `CAND=4 MODE=followup npx tsx spike.ts` (modes appended to spike.ts.txt; the generator code is unchanged). Started 13:59 +0530 with load average 34 / 56 / 46 and ended 14:00 with 22 / 51 / 45 (1 / 5 / 15 min), same machine and node. Times come from one pass and are noisy.
Method: each (N, L, seed) is built with an attempt bound of 500. The attempts it needs and its time are recorded, with performance.now() in the harness only. If a seed needs more than 30 attempts, it is also timed at bound 30, where it runs 30 failed attempts and then stops. "Smallest 0-run-out bound" is the largest number of attempts any seed in 1..1000 needed. At that bound every seed succeeds, so the worst time equals the worst time measured at bound 500.

Reading:
- No seed in 1..1000 is unreachable. Every run-out at 30 is a slow tail, not a dead end. Attempt counts vary a lot between seeds: p50 is 1 to 3, but (6,2) has p99 33 and max 84. A finite bound therefore lowers the run-out rate, but this sample does not show that any bound reaches zero for all 2^31 seeds. The tail keeps growing with the sample: the max is 47 over seeds 1..200 (seed 140) and 84 over seeds 1..1000.
- Cost per attempt is about 0.5 ms at (6,2) (84 attempts took 43 ms) and about 1.2 ms at (6,4) (42 attempts took 51 ms), under load.
  - A bound of about 100 stays near or under 50% of NFR-2. It would have no run-outs on this 1000-seed sample. That is not a guarantee.
  - A bound of 500 could cost about 600 ms for a slow (6,4) seed, which is over the 500 ms NFR-2 bound itself. 500 is not a safe choice.
- Conclusion: raising the bound to about 100 removes every run-out seen in seeds 1..1000 and keeps time inside the tripwire. It cannot guarantee "never". The page still needs a fallback for a run-out, for example a deterministic retry with the next seed, which is A-38's DL2 decision. The CLI with an explicit seed keeps the FR-86 error path.
- Any change to the 30 needs a signed change (FR-84, A-35). This section only measures; it does not change a bound.
